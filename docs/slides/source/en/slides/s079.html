Two loops stacked: angle outside, rate inside.

Outer loop: angle error × 15 → how fast to rotate back (a rate target).

Inner loop: rate target − gyro rate → the full PID → the mixer.

Why two loops? The fast rate loop fights quick bumps; the angle loop only decides how fast to come back.

Example

Angle 4°, target 0° → outer asks −60 °/s → gyro reads −20 °/s → inner error +40 → push harder back.

This is called a cascade. The outer loop works on angle and produces a rate target; the inner loop works on rate and produces motor corrections. Why two loops? The rate loop is fast and stiff, so it fights quick disturbances; the angle loop only has to decide how fast to come back to level.
