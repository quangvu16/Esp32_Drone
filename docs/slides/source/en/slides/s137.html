navSetpoints(): tilt toward the target

float ex = tgt_x - pos_x, ey = tgt_y - pos_y;
float dist = sqrtf(ex*ex + ey*ey);
if (dist < NAV_STOP_BAND_M || !tgt_valid) return;   // 0.6 m
float mag = constrain(dist * nav_kp, 0.0f, NAV_MAX_TILT_DEG);
float ux = ex / dist * mag;      // toward the target
float uy = ey / dist * mag;
// rotate into the body frame, then:
sp_pitch_deg = constrain(body_fwd,  -MAX_TILT_DEG, MAX_TILT_DEG);
sp_roll_deg  = constrain(body_left, -MAX_TILT_DEG, MAX_TILT_DEG);

A simple P controller: 1.2° of tilt per metre (nav_kp), capped at 12°. Inside 0.6 m, or with no target: stay level. RTH = target (0, 0).

Example: target 5 m ahead → 5 × 1.2 = 6° nose-down. 15 m ahead → 18 → capped at 12°.

Why cap at 12 degrees, less than the 25 degree manual limit? Navigation is the least certain part of the firmware, so it is allowed the gentlest moves. Why a stop band? The position estimate is never exact; without it the drone would hunt back and forth around a target it can never quite reach.
